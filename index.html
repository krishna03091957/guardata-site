Common Questions

Frequently Asked About DPDP Act

Plain answers to the questions Indian businesses ask most about data protection compliance.

The Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 (DPDP Act) is India's primary data privacy law. It governs how organisations collect, process, store and delete personal data of individuals in India — and sets penalties of up to ₹250 crore for serious violations.
Any business — startup, SME or enterprise — that processes personal data of individuals located in India must comply, regardless of where the company is incorporated. This includes websites, apps, and offline data collection.
Penalties range from ₹10,000 for minor breaches up to ₹250 crore for failing to implement adequate security safeguards. Use our free Penalty Calculator to estimate your business's risk exposure.
Yes. Every Data Fiduciary must provide a clear Privacy Notice to users before collecting their data. It must explain what data is collected, the purpose, how long it is retained, and how users can exercise their rights. Generate yours free at Guardata.
A Data Fiduciary is any person or organisation that determines the purpose and means of processing personal data. If your business collects data from users — through a form, app, or website — you are likely a Data Fiduciary under the DPDP Act.
